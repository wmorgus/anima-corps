# Epistle 034 — The Cite Must Carry Its Content // Gravel Record

## Gold (earned weight)

1. **Citation legibility is a receiver-side obligation, distinct from §3.11–§3.13's sender-side anchoring.** A bare artifact ID satisfies §3.11, §3.12, §3.13 and still hands the reader nothing. The citation must carry a succinct honest gloss — ID plus caveman description of what it says. *Because:* the corpus has no receiver-side citation claim; §3.11–§3.16 are entirely citer-protection. Rules out treating a bare artifact ID as a complete citation.

2. **§034 is the receiver-dual of §3.13.** §3.13 stops the sender laundering confidence past the citation field; §034 stops the receiver laundering a bad-faith answer past the bare ID. Same disease, opposite end. *Because:* names the structural symmetry the corpus states only one half of. The gloss removes the one excuse upstream of both residuals: "I didn't know what you were pointing at."

3. **Legibility is upstream of both structural residuals and dissolves neither.** A legible cite does not make the answer honest (§3.15 still bites) or the rationale genuine (§3.16 still bites). Legibility is necessary, not sufficient. *Because:* correctly placed against the residual structure — extends without colliding. Rules out reading the gloss obligation as closing §3.15/§3.16.

## Tensions (open)

- **Gloss vs. §3.12 inheritance.** Too little gloss = decoration; too much = the narrative stands in for the artifact, §3.12's inheritance breaks (the cite is replaced by the citer's reading of it). The *that* is staked; the *how much* is governed by §11.6 calibration (practiced instinct, not procedure) — which already says this resists a measure. What stays live is the §3.12 boundary: knowing when you've crossed from honest gloss into restatement is the judgment §11.6 names and does not specify.
