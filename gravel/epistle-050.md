## GOLD (earned weight)

1. **The retrieval problem is a granularity mismatch, not a search-quality problem; the section is the right grain to cite/supersede and the wrong grain to retrieve against.** *Because:* derives the diagnosis from §043 gold 2 (verified verbatim — the gravel record for 043 says "→ §3.3" has always cited the section, a §3.10 event has always touched the section), and §043 gold 2's confetti/§6.6 argument is reproduced accurately. The three failures (dilution, why-over-what weighting, pull-1500-use-40) are three statements of one cause, correctly framed. Load-bearing.

2. **Chunking the source is destructive in three named ways, all grounded in frozen claims.** *Because:* each leg verifies. Breaks §-addressing (the §-number addresses the section; chunks either collide on one address or invent sub-addresses the corpus never defined). Breaks the supersession unit — §043 gold 2's confetti, "arrived at from the other direction," is an exact and fair re-derivation. Breaks the frozen guarantee via §6a.8 (verified: §6a.8 is the frozen-tag-on-mutable-content category error; "one layer down" is a faithful application — agent-authored chunks standing in for a human_only frozen unit at retrieval time is structurally the same error). The "spine vs. service — do not damage the spine to speed up a service" framing is conviction prose that earns its place.

3. **The non-destructive alternative — an index beside the section — applies §2.10's retrieval-target split inward.** *Because:* §2.10 verified verbatim: "The correct corpus entry names *where to look*, not the timed fact itself. The retrieval target is a frozen invariant; the fetched content expires at the source, outside the corpus." The epistle's move — frozen invariant (the section) / lighter pointer-artifact (the index) — is a genuine structural reuse of §2.10, not a loose analogy. The territory/map framing is doxa but the underlying claim (point at the claim, do not chop the section) is the §2.10 move turned on the corpus itself. Earned.

4. **The index is the section's formal cause, junior to its final cause.** *Because:* §13.6 verified verbatim — "Final cause is primary... 'Final' is a nominal accident of the ordering; the load-bearing claim is the hierarchy, not the list." The epistle uses §13.6 correctly: it cites the hierarchy claim (not the flat list) and derives the index's juniority structurally — "it has direction over nothing on its own; strip the section and the index points at nothing." This is the §13.6a transit-pattern move (hierarchy one register down) applied honestly. The payoff — juniority is *why* the index can be regenerated/superseded/wrong without endangering the section — is the design constraint doing real work, not decoration. Load-bearing.

5. **Summary-plus-claims is the correct shape because it is the only one closed by the section.** *Because:* the four-criteria frame (single call / embeds well / claim granularity / survives supersession) is argued from what retrieval needs, not from what sounds useful. The tag-cloud rejection is correct and grounded: Calliope holds the relational structure as typed edges, and `elaborates` / `depends_on` / `supersedes` all verify as live `RelationshipType` enum members (types.py:1027-1036). "Build it as edges, traverse it as edges" honors the existing graph substrate. The Q&A rejection is the sharpest argument in the epistle: "closed by the section" vs. "open against the world of possible queries" is a real stability distinction, and "an index that decays independently of its source violates the regeneration contract" ties the shape choice to the production discipline below it. The closure argument is self-sufficient — it does not depend on the granularity bet (see Gravel 2). Earned.

6. **The envelope: derived, semi_liquid, version-pinned, `parent_artifact_id` to the section.** *Because:* `parent_artifact_id` verified as the live schema field (types.py:1073) carrying the exact CAL-04 contract the epistle cites — the comment reads "AC1: parent_artifact_id — must NOT be silently dropped." The epistle's rejection of inventing `source_artifact_id` is correct: that field is not in the schema, and `parent_artifact_id` carries exactly this meaning. The semi_liquid register is argued correctly ("stable until its source moves, then regenerated") and the comparison to `context_snapshot` and `backlog_item` verifies — `context_snapshot` is schema-forced to semi_liquid (types.py:1186-1190) and `backlog_item` is semi_liquid by convention (types.py:327). The `source_version: int` pin making staleness "detectable rather than silently wrong" is a clean property. Load-bearing.

7. **Regeneration-on-supersession is the non-negotiable half; the index has no independent lifecycle.** *Because:* §3.10 verified verbatim ("Supersession is Aufhebung: preserves, negates, elevates... Rules out: erasure; expiry; edit-in-place"). The discipline — "regenerated, never edited," `supersedes` edge from new index to old in lockstep with the section's own supersession — is the §3.10 commitment applied one register down, and it is what makes the index safe. "It moves when and only when its section moves" plus the `source_version` pin is the structural guarantee. This is the load-bearing safety argument and it holds.

8. **Index is the precondition for safe chunking, not a substitute argument against it.** *Because:* the order claim (know the claims, then decide whether to chunk) is genuinely earned — naive chunking is destructive *because* it chunks before knowing the claims, and the claims list is precisely that knowledge. The epistle does not overclaim: it explicitly says chunked artifacts would still be derived pointers into the frozen section, never replacements. This keeps the whole epistle consistent with its own anti-chunking-the-source position. Clean.

9. **One Home holds: the index lives in Calliope, not git.** *Because:* Epistle 039 (One Home) verified to exist (gravel/epistle-039.md present; cited in calliope/CLAUDE.md as the committed One-Home architecture). The argument is a correct application of an already-committed conviction. Earned.
