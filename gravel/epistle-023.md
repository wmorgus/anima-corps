# Epistle 023 — Constitutional cost of core change: Aufhebung, not erasure // Gravel Record

## Gold (earned weight)

**G1 — Core change is paid in coherence, not effort.** Change the zeroth-class telos and every *because* downstream is orphaned — the belt does not transfer (§13.13). After the change, exactly two states: re-derive everything, or stand with explicit orphans on the record. No third state. Silent orphans are the failure mode, not the price.
*Because:* §13.13 defines belt/hard core and degeneration but says nothing about the cost of changing the core or the orphan mechanism. Novel — stakes a cost-structure the corpus has no claim for.

**G2 — Compositional telos bounds blast radius — but only if the trace exists.** Telos is section-structured (Epistle 022, slot 4). Change is section-level; only artifacts deriving from the changed section are orphaned. The bound is real only with a derivation trace — no trace, no bound, and an untraceable section-change degrades silently into a whole-core change nobody scoped.
*Because:* novel extension of §13.13's belt model to bounded supersession. Makes targeted supersession possible without full replacement.

**G3 — No-erasure makes the cost auditable.** The orphan trail is the only evidence the G1 cost was paid rather than dodged. You cannot audit a re-derivation against a core you deleted.
*Because:* §3.10 stakes Aufhebung as the mutation primitive; it does not connect no-erasure to auditability of the cost of core change. That connection is the novel ore. (The bare restatement that supersession is Aufhebung / cancel-preserve-lift is gravel — see below.)

**G4 — Silent drift is a distinct failure from §13.13 degeneration.** Degeneration stays in-programme: belt thickens, core unchanged, the programme stops anticipating but flies its own banner honestly. Drift changes the programme and keeps the old telos in the header — a programme already become another, flying false colors. The telos doc is the fixed reference drift is measured against; with no stable zeroth-class reference, drift is undetectable by construction.
*Because:* §13.13 names degeneration and leaves it there. G4 names a second diachronic failure mode and distinguishes it sharply — and assigns the telos doc a job §13.13 didn't.

**G5 — Fork lives outside versioning and must be named.** CLAUDE.md semver (major = §-claim superseded) covers section-Aufhebung: expensive, bounded, continuous — a major bump. Full replacement — new telos, no derivation continuity — exceeds versioning entirely. Version implies continuity; a fork has none to imply. Calling a fork a version bump is the same laundering as erasing the orphan trail.
*Because:* the semver convention assumes continuity at every level. G5 names the act that has no continuity to version and the word it requires. Novel.

## Gravel (dropped)

**"Supersession of a telos section is Aufhebung — cancel/preserve/lift" as a bare statement.**
*Because:* duplicates §3.10 (Aufhebung: preserves/negates/elevates, rules out erasure), §13.7 (operation defined), §6.9 (extended to renames). The auditability link is kept as G3; the restatement is dropped.

**Genie / false-colors / scalpel-vs-detonation imagery.**
*Because:* rhetoric decoration, not new ore. Good epistle move — stays in the text, not smelted as a corpus claim.

## Tensions (open)

**Drift-reading cadence.** Inherited from §13.13's open: the fixed reference exists, the reading discipline does not. What cadence reads the supersession record at scale to catch drift remains unstaked. Not blocking.

**Fork-only sections.** Whether some telos sections are so coupled to the resolution that section-change is whole-core change in disguise. Genuinely open.

- Named the **unifying claim**: the constitutional-cost-of-core-change is one constraint with four faces (cost / mechanism / no-erasure / drift-reference). The epistle asserts the four are faces of one constraint without naming the constraint.
- **Sorted G3 from its own restatement** — the epistle braided the novel auditability link with a §3.10 duplicate; smelting separates them.
