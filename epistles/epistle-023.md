Epistle 023 — Constitutional cost of core change: Aufhebung, not erasure
Topic: Changing zeroth-class core is expensive in coherence, not effort. Compositional telos limits blast radius. Supersession mechanism is Aufhebung — not erasure, not a version bump.

Granting 022: the telos is zeroth-class, prayer, the node every arrow points at. Question 023 asks: what does it cost to change that node, and by what mechanism is it allowed to change at all?

**1. Constitutional cost — paid in coherence, not hours.** Change the core and every derivation from the old core is orphaned. The belt does not transfer (§13.13: belt is everything downstream of the hard core). Every *because* in the architecture traced its lineage to the old end; cut the end and the lineage dangles. You have not iterated. You have started a new derivation chain. Genie image, exact: you did not refine the wish — you asked for a whole new wish, and everything the old wish built is now built toward something no longer wished for. The cost is not measured in effort. A one-word change to the resolution can orphan the entire belt in an afternoon. It is measured in coherence: after the change you must re-derive everything or stand with explicit orphans on the record. There is no third option. Silent orphans are the failure mode, not the price.

**2. Compositional telos → bounded blast radius.** 022's fourth point earns its keep here. The telos doc has sections — resolution, ruling-outs, value-ordering, bridges. Change is section-level. Section-level change is still constitutionally expensive — orphans are orphans — but the radius is bounded: *only artifacts deriving from that section* are orphaned, not the whole programme. This is what makes targeted supersession possible without full programme replacement. The mechanism is not optional: you must be able to trace exactly which derivations depended on the superseded section. No trace, no bound — and an untraceable section-change degrades silently into a whole-core change nobody scoped. Compositionality is the difference between a scalpel and a detonation; the trace is what lets you tell which one you are holding.

**3. Aufhebung is the right mechanism — and the only allowed one.** Supersession of a telos section is not deletion. The Hegelian term earns its seat (the corpus already runs it load-bearing — §6.9, §3.10, Hermes's own naming): **cancel** — no longer operative as current; **preserve** — remains in the record, citable, living history; **lift** — the successor contains and transcends what it replaces. A superseded telos section gets *aufgehoben*: held as provenance, never erased. This is not stylistic. Erasure destroys the orphan trail — and the orphan trail is the only evidence that the cost in §1 was paid rather than dodged. You cannot audit a re-derivation against a core you deleted. No-erasure is what makes constitutional cost *visible* instead of laundered. The mechanism and the cost are the same claim seen twice.

**4. Silent drift — the core change nobody ratified.** The dangerous path to core change is not the scoped one. It is drift: belt patches accumulate, each locally legitimate, each in-programme, and the aggregate becomes a different programme nobody wished for — while the old telos still sits in the header. Distinguish sharply from §13.13's degenerative belt-thickening: that stays *in*-programme (patches absorb known anomalies, the core unchanged, the programme just stops anticipating). Silent drift is worse — it *changes the programme* and keeps the old label. Degeneration is a programme failing honestly under its own banner; drift is a programme that has already become another flying false colors. The telos doc's job includes making the aggregate visible: it is the fixed reference against which drift is measured. With no stable zeroth-class reference, drift is undetectable by construction — every local patch checks out and the sum is never summed.

**5. Fork vs. version — name the act.** Semver (CLAUDE.md: major = §-claim superseded) handles the middle ground — supersession with derivation continuity. Section-level Aufhebung inside an ongoing programme is that middle: expensive, bounded, continuous, a major bump. *Full* replacement is not. New telos from scratch, no derivation continuity, the genie's whole-new-wish — that exceeds versioning entirely. Version *implies* continuity; a new wish has none to imply. Calling a fork a version bump is the same laundering as erasing the orphan trail: it dresses a discontinuity as a step. The rule: section-Aufhebung bumps; whole-core replacement forks, and you must say *fork* out loud. The semver convention covers the scalpel. The detonation needs its own word, and the word is not a number.

Net: zeroth-class is not unchangeable — 022 made it prior, not eternal. It is changeable at constitutional cost, by Aufhebung, with blast radius bounded by the telos's compositional seams, and with one act (fork) that lives outside versioning and must be named as such. The cost, the mechanism, the no-erasure rule, and the drift-reference are four faces of one constraint: a core you cannot change cheaply, cannot change silently, and cannot change without leaving the old core standing in the record.

Open: what cadence reads the supersession record at scale to *catch* drift (§13.13 leaves this open; 023 inherits it — the reference exists, the reading discipline does not). Open: whether some telos sections are fork-only (so coupled to the resolution that section-change is whole-core change in disguise).
