Epistle 013 — The Vocabulary Corpus
Topic: Anima needs a declared vocabulary corpus — a dedicated file that is the definitional substrate the other corpora build on. Not a dictionary. Only terms Anima uses with load-bearing specificity: where the folk reading would mislead an agent reading cold. Population pass zero — bare essentials. Stakes the criterion, the ratification standard, the first population, and provenance as chain of custody.

**claim.** Vocabulary is a third kind of corpus. Logic has §-claims (conviction). Rhetoric has argument shapes (doxa). Vocabulary is definitional substrate — term resolution the other two presuppose and neither supplies. A dedicated file: `corpus_vocab.md`. Pass zero populates the terms already load-bearing in the corpora. The list is open; the ratification standard governs growth.

**why a third kind.** §20.2 stakes vocabulary compression as read-time efficiency — a precise term delivers act + object + orientation in one token. The compression only cashes out if the agent resolves the term to the Anima-specific meaning, not the folk one. Logic §-claims and rhetoric moves *use* terms like "shitcorp," "gravel," "epistle"; neither *defines* them. An agent reasoning from logic must already know what "shitcorp" rules out, or the §-claim is read wrong. The vocab corpus is what makes §20.2's compression payable: the table the reader resolves against. The logic corpus already names a domain-model register (the ratified vocabulary the other registers cite; see `corpora/anima/logic.md` register table, “Domain model / ubiquitous language” row) — but names it as a row, self-referentially folded into logic itself. This epistle extracts that function into its own artifact. Logic carries axioms; vocab carries symbols. The extract frees the logic corpus from the domain-model-register burden: logic *cites* the vocab corpus without *owning* it. A register folded into the conviction corpus could not be cited cleanly by rhetoric or ur-software without dragging logic's frame; the separate artifact can. Resolves what was T1.

**the ratification standard.** Not every term earns an entry. Test: does Anima use this term with enough specificity that the folk/common reading would mislead an agent reading cold? Load-bearing divergence from common usage, or obligation structure the agent needs to know cold (§1.5 — borrowed vocabulary carries obligation structure; the entry is where that obligation gets declared instead of silently imported). If the common reading is close enough, no entry — the term resolves correctly without help, and an entry would be inflation. The bar is divergence, not importance. A term can be important and common (no entry); a term can be minor and divergent (entry earned). This cuts against dictionary instinct: the vocab corpus is small by construction. Terms enter through epistles — the same ratification loop that governs everything else. No separate mechanism: the loop is already the mechanism. This epistle is the first example. Resolves what was T3.

**entry shape.** Each entry carries four things: (1) the term; (2) the compressed Anima-specific definition; (3) what it rules out — the folk reading or near-synonym the entry exists to block; (4) provenance — the §-claim, epistle, gravel record, agent definition (`.claude/agents/*.md`), or `CLAUDE.md` that grounded the meaning. Same shape as a logic §-claim minus the conviction force: a definition is not a stake about the world, it is a coordinate for reading stakes about the world.

Agent definitions and `CLAUDE.md` are valid provenance because they are the operational substrate — the documents that define how the system runs. The terms they define ("epistle," "gold," "gravel," "reach," the status lifecycle) are real and load-bearing. Two kinds of firmness, not one. A term grounded in `lite.md` / `CLAUDE.md` carries operational-substrate firmness: real, load-bearing, valid provenance — the chain of custody holds. A term grounded in a §-claim carries ratified-corpus firmness: frozen under the §3 ratification cycle. The provenance-validity is the same; the ratification status is not. Naming the asymmetry here is the same cut the kind signal makes below (operational vs. epistemic vocabulary) — the two claims reinforce each other.

**provenance source = vocabulary kind signal.** The provenance source tells an agent what *kind* of vocabulary it is reading. Terms grounded in agent definitions / `CLAUDE.md` → operational vocabulary: germane to builders, to how the system runs. Terms grounded in §-claims, epistles, gravel → epistemic vocabulary: germane to reasoners, to how the corpus holds. Same vocab corpus, two provenance registers, two audiences. An agent reads the citation and knows whether a term is its problem. Load-bearing: it lets agents filter the vocab corpus by what's relevant to their job without reading the whole thing. The provenance field does double duty — chain of custody (below) and audience routing.

The "rules out" clause does different work in a vocab entry than in a §-claim. A §-claim's "rules out" blocks a world-position (conviction). A vocab entry's "rules out" blocks a folk reading (disambiguation). Different targets, same syntax. No drift risk: the distinction is held by structure — §-numbered claim vs. definition entry — not by argument. Agents must not conflate the two registers; the structure tells them which they are reading. Resolves what was T2.

No comparative argumentation inside an entry. No "why not refine?" The definition must be precise enough to earn its place without extra words. The "rules out" clause is the sharpening instrument: short, semantic, blocks the specific misreading. If a definition cannot block its folk reading without added explanation, the definition is wrong — revise the definition, don't append the argument. The test, exactly: definition + rules-out + provenance, no more. Anything past that is the dictionary instinct leaking back in.

**pass zero — bare essentials.** First population = terms already in use and already load-bearing:
- *shitcorp* — the immune act: identify accidental garbage → remove → cite the because. Rules out: curate/filter/refine (lower-bandwidth synonyms); janitorial or aesthetic reading. [§20.3, §20.4, §3.20, epistle-012]
- *epistle* — staked direction submitted to shitcorping; not an argument. Rules out: essay, proof, position paper that argues its case. [`CLAUDE.md` — epistle format, status lifecycle, staked-direction framing]
- *gravel / shitcorpus* — the via-negativa aggregate; permanent record of what was dropped and why. Rules out: trash (gravel is evidence); scratch notes. [§6.6, §3.20]
- *gold / gravel (as shitcorping outputs)* — gold = earned weight; gravel = dropped, with because. Rules out: pass/fail; gold as praise rather than load-bearing-survival. [`.claude/agents/lite.md` — operational definitions of panning outputs]
- *vapor / liquid / semi-liquid / frozen* — epistle status states, each a relational stance: not what is happening to the epistle, but how an agent behaves toward it. *vapor* = pre-artifact; reason from the conversation only, no epistle to cite. *liquid* = iterating artifact; engage, revise, push back; do not yet reason from as settled. *semi-liquid* = agreed-but-uncommitted; shitcorping accepted, corpus changes pending; hold as candidate, do not ratify yet. *frozen* = provenance artifact; gravel committed, corpus updated; cite and reason from. The lifecycle applies the §1.6/§2.5 relational gradient to epistles — it does not re-stake the gradient as a process ladder. Rules out: draft/review/final (these track process, not the stance the artifact calls for); conflating semi-liquid (hold as candidate) with liquid (still engage and revise). [§1.6, §2.5 — the relational liquidity gradient (how to behave toward an artifact) the lifecycle applies to epistles; CLAUDE.md — four-state epistle lifecycle definition]
- *§-claim* — a numbered conviction-corpus claim carrying what it rules out. Rules out: assertion without a rules-out; a citation target without conviction force. [`corpora/anima/logic.md` format — the §-numbered conviction-corpus entry form; the corpus defines by demonstrating]
- *reach* — a claim that grasps past what it earned. Rules out: error (a reach can be directionally right); the reach is named in shitcorping, not deleted. [`.claude/agents/lite.md` — "reasoning that arrives at the edge of something real and stops"]
- *corpus* — a domain reasoned-*from*, frozen as coordinates. Rules out: documentation; a domain reasoned-*about*. [§6.6]

List not closed. The standard, not this enumeration, decides additions.

**provenance is chain of custody, not decoration.** Each entry traces to its source. A term whose meaning floats free of a §-claim or epistle is a definition without a because — structurally the §3.20 failure mode (the uncited discard) applied to definitions: a coordinate that cannot be checked against what grounded it. Provenance is what makes the vocab corpus trustworthy to reason *from*. Without it, the corpus is just assertion about what words mean — and an agent reading from it would launder that assertion as authority (the §3.15 staleness pattern, applied to vocabulary). The provenance link is also the colonization tripwire (§15.12, §15.16): when a vocab entry borrows a term from another corpus (logic ← ur-software, e.g.), the provenance trail makes the crossing visible. A term arriving without provenance is colonization laundered as elaboration.

**tensions.** None open. The three the draft surfaced are closed, not dropped:
- T1 — domain-model self-reference vs. extracted vocab file. Resolved: extract. Logic carries axioms and *cites* the vocab corpus; vocab carries symbols and *owns* them. Folded into "why a third kind."
- T2 — entry "rules out" vs. §-claim "rules out" drift. Resolved: different targets (folk reading vs. world-position), distinction held by structure not argument. Folded into "entry shape."
- T3 — pass-zero closure / who applies the standard. Resolved: terms enter through epistles, the existing ratification loop; no separate mechanism. Folded into "the ratification standard."
