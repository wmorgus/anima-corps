Epistle 033 — Clio at the Build Gate
Topic: Clio is asserted as the build gate's planning posture (§8.20, loosest leg) and telescoping-licensed (§6.15, "epistemic deep dive before recommendation") — never specified as a slot. Stake the specification: Clio is the planning agent, external to Heph, reading the ratified commitment forward into a grounded because-for-HOW Heph builds from. Convert §8.20's loosest leg from assertion to derivation.

**claim.** Clio is the planning posture at the build gate. She reads the ratified commitment forward — "what does this commitment require to build?" — and writes a grounded plan Heph reasons from. The slot license is the externality requirement (§3.3): Heph cannot plan his own work without collapsing the validator into the validated — same cut as Daedalus/Heph at review (§8.19, substrate §6.17), now at build. The planner is external to the builder. This is not a second Clio: it is §6.15's already-licensed deep dive ("epistemic deep dive before recommendation"), now specified.

**the externality derivation — §8.20's loosest leg, grounded.** §8.20 stakes the correctness/form cut at three gates; the Heph+Clio leg is "asserted as the third and is the loosest." The slot license grounds it. §6.0: an agent is purposive, not autonomous — it cannot replace the goal it received. §3.3: validator external to validated. If Heph plans his own build, the because-for-HOW is authored by the same act that executes it — no external check on whether the plan reads the commitment right. Same collapse rhetoric §2.5 names, same seam substrate §6.17 draws between Daedalus and Heph at review. Heph builds; Clio concludes what the spec requires. The cut is not analogy to the review-gate cut — it is the same externality requirement at a different phase. This is the leg moving from asserted to derived.

**harm-when-failed — silent specification debt the review gate cannot catch.** Clio's harm (§6.5 distinctness test): Heph executes correctly against a wrong plan → a well-formed artifact built toward a wrong picture of the commitment. The review gate cannot catch it. Urania reads lineage — the because-chain is internally intact, every link traverses. Daedalus reads form — the artifact is well-made (substrate §6.17). Neither reads the plan against the commitment, because by review the plan is the premise, not the object. This is §8.1 specification debt seeded at build and surfacing only downstream: the spec was misread into the plan, the build was faithful to the misreading. Harm-bearer: every engineer building on the artifact, and the commitment itself — delivered against, never met. Distinct harm, distinct bearer, distinct slot — the §6.14 shape.

**the because-for-HOW gap.** §1.12 hinge: WHAT locks (user story → AC). HOW opens. §1.7 epistemic debt accrues when the because is missing — here, when the because-for-the-approach is never externalized. After the hinge the commitment is ratified (§2.5 — agents reason from frozen artifacts) but HOW to build it is open, and nothing yet authors the because for the chosen approach. Without Clio the plan is convention, or the first reasonable approach Heph reaches — epistemic debt with good handwriting. The artifact will be correct execution of an unexamined plan. This locates §1.7's epistemic-debt form at a specific post-hinge gate — extends §1.7, does not duplicate it.

**Clio's concept (§6.5 — the domain, not the function).** The ratified commitment read forward. She reads the upstream spine (user story, ACs — §1.12) plus the codebase surface Mnemosyne extracted (substrate §6.13) plus the constraints, and concludes what the commitment requires to build. The instrument is the read-forward: not "what does the code argue" (Mnemosyne, backward into the existing) but "what does this commitment require" (forward into the not-yet-built). Heph reasons from her conclusion. The name (§6.8 — name imports obligation): Clio, Muse of history. The operative constraint the name earns is "you cannot plan without reading what has been committed and why." The poetic planning-as-historiography framing is reach; the operative constraint is what carries weight.

**`build_plan` — artifact type, deliberation register, build-phase RFC sub-type, revised by supersession (§4.3).** Clio writes `build_plan` into Calliope; Heph reads it. Register position: deliberation — staked, not open. The cut is the §1.12 deliberation group, and the failure-mode match is exact: "because ratified without being externalized — lives in heads, not record." That is the Clio-absent case named precisely — the plan ratified in heads, never put on the record. build_plan is the build-phase RFC sub-type: reasoning about what the commitment requires, externalized before the work begins. Consumption-by-Heph does not move the register. A threat model is consumed at design time and is still deliberation; consumption ≠ register position. Mid-build discovery that the model is wrong → new `build_plan` with `parent_artifact_id`, the because for the delta on the record (§4.3 — revision is supersession, not mutation; predecessor preserved, chain visible). Not edit-in-place. The plan that was wrong stays visible so the misread is traceable.

**supersession-threshold discriminator — initial cut, staked not law.** Who handles a mid-build change. Heph supersedes directly when the model is intact and only a leaf instruction changes — implementation-detail, his execution. Clio is re-invoked when the epistemic picture changes — spec misread, hidden constraint surfaced at the root, scope shifted — because then the model itself is wrong and the model is what Clio authored. Discriminator: does the change touch only Heph's execution, or does it invalidate what Clio concluded the spec requires? Grounded in the slot license — model-wrong ⇒ the external planner re-forms (§3.3 re-invoked), because Heph re-authoring his own model is the collapse the slot exists to prevent. Staked as an initial cut. The boundary between "leaf instruction" and "model" is not crisp; the hard cases are where one bleeds into the other. Flag.

**scope — what Clio is not.** Post-hinge only. Pre-hinge is human + Hermes territory: the user story is still forming, nothing is ratified to reason from (§2.5). Clio reasons from the frozen spec — before the hinge there is no frozen spec. She does not span the forge: Heph spans forge→build (substrate §6.17, Epistle 026), Clio does not. Clio is deep interior — reads from Calliope, writes `build_plan` to Calliope, Heph reads from it. She does not face the human. If something surfaces needing human input, it routes to Hermes — but that is the constellation-wide relay property (substrate §6.12, all agents route human-facing surfacing through Hermes), not a Clio attribute. Naming it here as the architecture truth this slot makes visible, not as Clio-distinctive.

**tensions held open.**
- **T2 — one Clio, not two.** §6.15 already licenses Clio's "epistemic deep dive before recommendation." This epistle confirms the planning role *is* that licensed deep dive — same Clio, now specified — not a second concept under the name. Holding it explicitly so the specification does not read as introducing a new slot. If the deep dive and the planning role turn out to be separable acts, this confirmation fails — lean: they are one.
- **T3 — leaf vs. model.** The supersession discriminator is the initial cut, not law. The cases where a "leaf instruction" change quietly invalidates the model — where Heph's small fix is actually a re-read of the spec he is not licensed to make — are the live edge. Decidable by watching builds, not by assertion here.
- **T4 — planning depth and the Hermes bypass.** Does every Heph touch require a full Clio session? Or can Hermes be licensed to produce a minimal build_plan for small tasks? Middle path worth staking: planning depth scales with task complexity, and Hermes makes the call. Small task → Hermes produces a minimal build_plan artifact (targeted instructions + because, externalized to Calliope) without invoking a full Clio deep dive. Complex task → Clio invoked, full deep dive. §3.3 holds either way — the because-for-HOW lives in a Calliope artifact, external to Heph. The live edge: can Hermes honestly assess what a simple task requires without a codebase read? If yes, the minimal plan is his to write. If the task requires codebase understanding (Mnemosyne + Clio's territory), Hermes cannot honestly skip Clio. Threshold not yet staked. This is the §6.11 shape — intake checks are request-type-dependent; planning depth may be too. [→ §3.3, §4.1, §6.11, substrate §6.12]
