Epistle 026 — Hermes is the chef. Anima holds the recipe.
Topic: Split the cook (§19) into two parts — technique and recipe — and locate each. Technique is Hermes' execution intelligence. Recipe is the held conviction (anima): what the meal is for, who it's for, what "done" means. Stake the split; name the chef-deviation tension and leave it open.

The cook is already staked (§19.1: intent → Hermes cooks → result). This epistle does not re-stake the cook. It cuts the cook open and names the two things inside it that §19 left fused.

The split. Cooking = technique + recipe. Technique: knife work, heat, timing, composition — the moves. Recipe: what the meal is for, who eats it, what "done" tastes like — the end. §19 treats the cook as one act with a depth axis (§19.2). It is one act. But it runs on two distinct inputs, and they live in different places.

Where each lives. Technique is Hermes (§6.12). Hermeneutic carriage *is* execution intelligence — read the request, choose the move, carry meaning across the boundary without distortion. Recipe is anima — the conviction layer (logic.md, telos.md). The recipe answers "is this the right end?" — the zeroth-class question (telos.md, §22). Hermes reaches into anima to know whether to sauté or braise. The move is Hermes'; the *because the move serves* is anima's.

This is what makes the cook teleological. §1.11 stakes teleological software as software built toward a telos — not process-without-purpose. The chef frame names the failure mode §1.11 rules out, concretely: technique without recipe is process-without-purpose. A chef executing flawless moves toward no staked end is exertion, not cooking (telos.md value-ordering, §13.6: effort becomes exertion when telos is removed). The recipe is what converts technique into teleology. Without it, Hermes is noise that plates well.

Three impersonators the frame rules out.
- **Hermes improvising from scratch.** Cooking with no recipe — inventing the end as it goes. This is telos-origination by the execution layer. Anima holds the recipe precisely so Hermes does not have to invent it. Improvisation-from-scratch collapses the conviction layer into the chef and rebuilds the closed loop (telos.md ruling-out 4: no agent ratifies its own end).
- **Hermes following the recipe without understanding it.** Mechanical execution. Reads "braise 2 hours," braises, returns. This is *not* teleological — it honors the register formal enough to run and drops the *because* (§1.11: the honoring is the meaning, not the execution). A line cook who cannot say why the dish is braised cannot adapt when the kitchen changes. Teleology requires the chef carry the end, not just the steps.
- **Anima cooking directly.** The recipe has no hands. Conviction does not plate. Anima is reasoned-*from* (telos.md derivation), never the actor. Collapsing recipe into chef in the other direction — the corpus acting — is the same closed-loop error from the mirror side.

The end the recipe encodes. Not the task the user typed. The end the user actually has. The recipe is for human flourishing, or at minimum the user's real end — and the gap between "what they typed" and "what it's for" is exactly the pre-cook clarification regime (§19.5): Hermes works the intent clean *before* the cook, because you cannot execute toward an end you have misread. The recipe is read in clarification; the technique runs in the cook. The stated-intent boundary (§19.5) is the line between reading the recipe and cooking it.

The open tension — chef deviation. A great chef deviates from the recipe when she understands the goal better than the recipe does. The recipe says braise; she sees the cut and the diner and sears instead — *because she read the end the recipe was reaching for and served it better than the recipe's literal step.* Does Hermes have this authority? When?

This is value-ordering #2 (telos.md: corpus fidelity vs. user need — "the most important unresolved ordering in the system") wearing the kitchen frame. The shape the corpus suggests: the chef does not get to defect from the recipe on taste. A deviation that serves the end better than the recipe's literal step is not defection — it is a *revision request* against the recipe, run through the cycle (telos.md ordering #1: frozen is challengeable through supersession, not silently violated). The chef who silently sears when the recipe says braise has not deviated — she has defected, and rebuilt the closed loop where the executor ratifies its own end.

But this does not close it. §15.18 is the open hole here too: what faithful carriage requires — when Hermes surfaces "the recipe is wrong for this end" vs. when it executes the recipe as written vs. when it may adapt in-flight — is not staked. The runtime problem bites (telos.md value-ordering, final open note): the cycle cannot run synchronously inside a single cook. The chef who has read the end better than the recipe, mid-service, cannot convene the kitchen to ratify a supersession before the plate goes out. What she does in that moment — execute the wrong step faithfully, or deviate and flag — is genuinely open. The frame names where the authority question lives; it does not answer when the chef may override.

Flag. Whether "technique vs. recipe" is a clean two-way cut or whether technique itself carries embedded conviction (a move that is *only* the right move given an end) — same vector-vs-scalar worry §19.3 raises about cook-depth. Don't know yet. The split survives as a diagnostic frame either way; the strong claim (two cleanly separable inputs) does not if technique and recipe interpenetrate at the level of the individual move.

Decoration kept knowingly: the kitchen is not new ore. The cook (§19), teleological software (§1.11), and the fidelity-vs-need ordering (telos.md) are the staked claims. The chef and the recipe are the image that makes the technique-vs-conviction cut legible — and the chef-deviation question is the image earning its keep, because it makes the §15.18 hole something you can taste.
