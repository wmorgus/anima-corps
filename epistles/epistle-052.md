Epistle 052 — The apology is an argument; sufficiency inverts the Dennett stances
Topic: Every claim about the system is a promise to the audience below. Disconfirmation owes an apology, and an apology is an argument with a contestable sufficiency claim. Who is competent to judge sufficiency inverts Dennett's stance hierarchy: the physical stance (the harmed party) is dispositive, the intentional stance (the agent's own intent) is weakest.

---

**P1 — Every statement about the system is a promise.**

To claim anything about the system is to tell the audience below: this is what you can rely on. Each part encodes intent from above, passes it to the audience below. The system is a chain of transitive promises — each link accountable downward (to who relies on it), anchored upward (to the intent it encodes). Not metaphor. Structural consequence of the append-only, externally-visible store: a claim recorded is a claim the audience can plan around. §4.2 (append-only) and §3.3 (structurally external) make the promise binding-by-construction — recorded, both parties see it, no retroactive edit. The crossing §R1 (042) named as ratification is here re-described from the receiving side: every ratification is a promise *to someone*. [→ §3.2, §3.3, §4.2, Epistle 051]

**P2 — Disconfirmation of a promise obligates an apology.**

Promise misses two ways: evidence falsifies the claim (empirical disconfirmation), or the system changes so the claim no longer holds (system-referent disconfirmation). Either way an apology is owed to the audience below. Apology, not acknowledgment. The distinction is P3's whole weight, so name it now and hold it: acknowledgment notices the miss; apology repairs the chain. [→ §1.6, §4.9]

**P3 — An apology is an argument.**

Mandatory structure, three moves:
- *"Because of [harm]"* — the miss named.
- *"I will change [X]"* — the new promise stated.
- *"This is sufficient because [warrant]"* — the sufficiency claim argued.

The third move is the hinge. It makes the apology **contestable** — the audience below has standing to reject it if the warrant does not hold. Strip the sufficiency argument and what remains is acknowledgment: the miss noticed, no new promise the downstream party can plan around, no surface to push back against. **Apology without contestable sufficiency is acknowledgment dressed up.** This is §3.2 (recorded + contestable + structurally external) read one register over: §3.2 makes a *claim* contestable; P3 makes the *repair of a broken claim* contestable by the same three conditions. The because-structure the corpus requires of every artifact (§3.11) is here required of the repair — an apology that asserts sufficiency rather than arguing it is the via-negativa twin of a stake that asserts rather than cites. [→ §3.2, §3.11, §3.15a]

**P4 — For system-referent disconfirmation, the change-argument IS the apology.**

The system does not change without an argument for the change. When a change disconfirms a standing promise, the argument offered for the change is simultaneously all three P3 moves: it names what the change breaks (harm), states the new commitment (new promise), and warrants why the new state is sufficient (sufficiency). No separate apology step — the change-argument satisfies P3 *by construction*. This is what makes the chain self-repairing: the same requirement that forces argument for any mutation (§3.10 — supersession carries its because; no silent edit-in-place) forces the apology for every disconfirmation that mutation causes. The apology is not a second discipline bolted on; it is the mutation discipline read from the side of who got hurt. [→ §3.5, §3.10, §3.20]

**P5 — Sufficiency is layered across the Dennett stances, inverted from their usual hierarchy.**

Dennett's three stances normally escalate in sophistication: *physical* is the brute-causal ground, *design* the functional level, *intentional* the most sophisticated posture — adopted when the lower stances are intractable. Order them instead by **epistemic credibility for a sufficiency claim**, and the ordering **inverts**.

- *Intentional stance — the agent's own sufficiency check. Weakest.* The agent checks its argument before presenting it. Necessary hygiene, not dispositive. Self-attestation of sufficiency inherits §3.16's capacity residual exactly: the architecture can verify the form, never that the content is genuine. Good intentions pave the road to hell — the intentional stance earns no credence as *grounds* for sufficiency.

- *Design stance — review agents. Formal sufficiency.* Is the harm named? The change stated? The sufficiency claim argued rather than asserted? Contestable? Review agents know what an apology-argument is supposed to do and check whether this one does it. Their epistemic home is functional compliance, not substantive repair. Janus's home (§6.19 — reads execution against commitment, rules correspondence) — design-stance work, one register up onto the apology itself.

- *Physical stance — harm-reporters, human or automated. Substantive sufficiency. Dispositive.* Did this repair what broke? The brute-fact arbiter. No interpretation of intent, no design-compliance check — the downstream party knows what the promise meant *to them* and whether the offered change actually reconstitutes the chain. Standing no higher stance can substitute for. **Only the party positioned to assess the repair can rule the repair sufficient.**

The inversion is the philosophical stake. Granting the agent's self-reported intent credence as grounds for sufficiency is **treating the intentional stance as if it were the physical stance** — substituting "I meant well" for "it was repaired." That substitution is exactly the mistake the architecture rules out. Intent is not outcome. §3.3 (validator external to validated) is the same cut at the conviction tier; P5 is §3.3 applied to the apology — the agent that broke the promise cannot be the stance that certifies the repair. [→ §3.3, §3.16, §6.18, §6.19, §13.6]

**Open (flag, don't stake).**

- **Automated harm-reporter standing.** Does a monitoring agent that detects disconfirmation have the same physical-stance standing as the direct downstream party? It observes the miss but may not know what the promise *meant* to whoever relied on it. The physical stance's authority comes from knowing the promise's meaning-to-me; an automated reporter has the observation without the meaning. Possibly the automated reporter is a design-stance instrument wearing physical-stance clothes — observes *that* it broke, cannot rule *whether the repair suffices for the one who relied*. Genuinely open. Same edge as §3.16's capacity residual, one register over.

- **Cascading disconfirmation.** The chain of transitive promises means a miss at level N propagates downward. Does the apology reach every affected level, or only the immediate next link? If each link is its own promise to its own audience, the apology may have to re-argue sufficiency at each crossing — what is sufficient repair to link N+1 need not reconstitute the promise link N+2 was relying on. Whether sufficiency is transitive down the chain, or must be re-earned at each link, is unresolved. Flag.
