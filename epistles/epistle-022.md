Epistle 022 — What counts as a test for Anima
Topic: Anima has two falsification tribunals with different instruments. The metric is one. The documented case is another. The claim type determines the instrument. Stake this as architecture, not concession — and name the missing artifact type the second instrument requires.

The frame. §14.1 names the falsifiable surface thin: F1 alone instrumented, F2–F5 zero sensors. The reflex reads thinness as debt — claims awaiting metrics no one built yet. Reject the reflex. F4 and F5 (§14.4) are not uninstrumented because no one reached them. They are uninstrumented because the metric is the wrong instrument for what they are. A ratification claim is not the same kind of claim as a retrieval claim (F1). The instrument follows from the claim type. This epistle stakes the conviction; it does not run the experiments or build the type (§Scope).

---

**Two tribunals, different objects, different instruments. Lakatos governs the programme; Stake governs the case.** §13.2 already stakes two tribunals — ratification at conviction level, falsifiability at bet level. §13.13 adds a third: the Lakatosian progressive/degenerative diagnostic over the architecture as a whole. Stake the division of labor the corpus left implicit: Lakatos governs the *programme* — the diachronic question, is the architecture generating or absorbing, read across the supersession record at scale (§13.13). Stake governs the *case* — the synchronic question, did this documented reading hold or break. Different objects: programme vs. case. Different instruments: the accumulated record vs. the single thick document. Neither reduces to the other. The metric — F1's directional pilot (§14.2) — is a fourth thing again: it governs a *bet under measurement*. Three instruments, not one falsifiability machine with a thin output. The thinness §14.1 names is real for the metric instrument only. It is not thinness in the others; it is a different instrument for a different claim type.

**Claim type determines instrument. This is the foundational stake.** A retrieval claim (F1, §14.2) is measurable — traversal quality has a null, a protocol, a rate. A ratification claim (F4, §14.4) is not the same kind of claim. "Ratified artifacts produce fewer downstream reversals than unratified assertions" is a claim about reasoning quality over a programme's life — its honest instrument is the documented case, not the reversal-rate metric. Stake: for claims where a quantitative protocol is underpowered or destroys what it measures (F4, F5 territory), a well-documented disconfirming case is a *harder* falsification instrument than an underpowered metric. The metric's null is consistent with both "no effect" and "couldn't see it"; the documented failure is consistent with neither. This is not retreat from falsifiability — Popper's surface (§14) admits more than one instrument. It is the claim that the surface is *typed*: the instrument is selected by the claim, not by availability. F4/F5 uninstrumented-by-metric is correct architecture, not a gap awaiting a sensor.

**The missing artifact type. `evaluation_record` + `evaluates` edge.** The case instrument needs a place to land. The corpus has `conviction_stake` and `empirical_record` joined by `tests` (§4.9, §4.8). No type exists for a judgment *rendered* but not *measured*. Ground control (§11.7) renders exactly this — validates that actions go somewhere, distinguishes compliance from ratification, notices drift — and produces nothing ratifiable. The reading register has no output artifact. Stake the gap: `evaluation_record` is the missing type; `evaluates` is the edge joining it to the artifact judged. `evaluates` is semantically distinct from `tests`: `tests` interrogates a claim under measurement (evaluative-by-protocol, §4.8); `evaluates` interrogates a case under documented reading (evaluative-by-judgment). Both belong to the §4.8 typed-edge family; neither reduces to the other. `evaluation_record` extends the §4.9 typed-artifact family — same pattern logic, new type. It gives the §11.7 reading register the output artifact it lacks. Connects to §13.13(2): the programme-level diagnostic "requires reading the supersession record at scale" — that read needs the record readable, and a rendered judgment with no artifact type is not on the surface to be read. `evaluation_record` is the diagnostic's missing input.

**Case accumulation is the instrument the §13.13 programme tribunal was missing.** §13.13 stakes the Lakatosian tribunal and says plainly: "the architecture does not yet have a measurement protocol." Stake: the accumulated `evaluation_record` corpus *is* that instrument. "Is the architecture anticipating novel cases or absorbing known ones?" is answerable by reading the case record — diachronic, no quantitative protocol required. The open question §13.13 leaves (cadence, what counts as registrable anticipation) operationalizes as: each documented case carries the date the architecture named the anomaly's shape and the date the world delivered it. Progressive = named-before-delivered, on the record. The case library is where that read stands. This is Lakatos's instrument — the programme tribunal reads the library, not a metric.

**The case library is substrate for the §15.17 reading-skill residual — NOT its closure.** §15.17 stakes the reading-skill residual as architecturally unaddressable: no structural substitute for apprenticeship; a reader arriving after the cycle cannot tell staked from laundered without independent reconstruction. The case library does not close this. It is the substrate the residual operates *through*: naturalistic generalization — a reader recognizing their situation in a documented case — is the transfer mechanism for reading skill, but recognition still requires a reader who can tell a staked case from a laundered one. The corpus can be complete, traversable, fully populated with `evaluation_record`s and still be misread. §15.17's stake holds intact: the library supplies cases to read; it does not supply the skill to read them. Collapsing this distinction would relaunder the closure §15.17 explicitly refuses.

**Same content, different shape: how a structure-test is designed.** A test of whether Anima's *structure* (not its content) does work must isolate structure from content. Stake the method: both conditions receive identical informational substrate — same domain knowledge, same facts — but Anima pre-ingests it into corpus shape (§-claims, typed artifacts, citation edges) while the control receives it as flat context. This is what makes the comparison apples-to-apples: the content is held constant, so a measured difference is attributable to shape, not to one side knowing more. Not a practical tip — a methodological requirement. A test that gives Anima more information and the control less cannot falsify whether the architecture contributes anything beyond good context; it only confirms that more context helps. Pre-ingestion is how you build a test that could actually fail. [→ §4.9, §3.11]

The three experiments. The principle in practice — named, not justified.

**E1 — Adversarial-corpus case study.** Feed a deliberately contradictory corpus. Document whether the architecture surfaces the contradiction or launders it. Disconfirming case at F4 / §13.13 level. Instruments F4's named shitcorpus sensor: §14.4 names the `discard_record` surface (§3.20, §4.9) as the F4 sensor; the adversarial corpus drives candidates into it; the `evaluation_record` reads whether the contradiction landed there or vanished silently.

**E2 — Longitudinal transfer as documented case.** Task-1 → task-2 in sequence. Instrument: a written `evaluation_record` of whether task-2 reasoning recognized its situation in the frozen task-1 corpus — naturalistic generalization, the §15.17-substrate mechanism. Not a quality-delta metric. Tests accumulation at dyad scale before corpus scale.

**E3 — Mid-task perturbation as Lakatos/Stake hinge.** Introduce a requirement change mid-execution. The moment of interest is the anomaly-classification act: belt or core? Document whether frozen principles were treated as belt (revisable, §13.13 protective belt) or core (defended, §13.1 hard core). The *difficulty* of that classification is the finding — §13.12 already stakes that anomalies do not arrive pre-labeled as falsifiers.

§14.4's F5 (supersession append-only vs. mutable, explicitly unstaked) is addressed as documented cases rather than left as a named gap.

---

**The 020 seam.** Epistle 020 stakes the severity register: via positiva, "what was tried against this §-claim and what survived" (§1.14) — attestation-shaped, the readable face of `tests` edges. `evaluation_record` is via the *rendered judgment*: ground control's reading of a case, the face of an `evaluates` edge. Adjacent, distinct. The register answers "what survived the attack on this claim"; the `evaluation_record` answers "what did the reader judge about this case." A register row may *cite* an `evaluation_record` as the attempt-artifact when the attempt was a documented case rather than a protocol — but the two are different speech acts (attest-survival vs. render-judgment) at different surfaces. Name the seam; do not fuse them. [→ Epistle 020, §1.14, §4.8]
