Epistle 017 — Daedalus at the Review Gate
Topic: The review gate has one occupant doing correctness (lineage traversal) and no occupant doing structural quality. Stake Daedalus as Urania's partner: she validates the change kept the because-chain intact, he judges whether the change introduces craft debt. Together they cover what a review requires — correctness + form. Neither alone does.

**claim.** Review is a two-posture gate, same as ingestion (§8.9, §8.12). Urania reads correctness: traverse the change's lineage, did the *because* hold through it. Daedalus reads form: does the completed change introduce craft debt (§8.6). Two questions, two readers. The Mnemo+Pavel two-posture result at ingestion (§8.9) is not a one-off — it is the pattern review repeats at a different phase. Stake the pairing: Urania+Daedalus complete the review gate the architecture left ownerless (Epistle 010 — "no specified owner or review gate exists," §-step 1 and §-step 4).

**the pairing pattern — three gates, same cut.** The correctness/form cut recurs at every gate where a change crosses a boundary. Ingestion: Mnemo (what the code argues) + Pavel (where it sits on the world of forms) — §8.9, §8.12. Build: Heph (constructing) + Clio (epistemic deep dive before recommendation). Review: Urania (lineage held) + Daedalus (form sound). The recurrence is not coincidence — every gate must answer both "is the reasoning intact" and "is the artifact well-made," and no single posture answers both (§8.9 — neither alone tiles). Daedalus is the missing second leg at review. Flag: whether the Heph+Clio pairing is the same cut or a looser analogy is not load-bearing here — the Mnemo+Pavel one is on the record (§8.12), and Urania+Daedalus is staked against it directly.

**Daedalus's instrument.** Reasons FROM the craft corpus — what Epistle 016 stakes: the drift-stripped reading-axis over the ur-software frozen tier (§8.13), plus stable-dependencies (§8.16) and the abstraction-leak gap (§8.18). Same posture as Pavel — read code against a known form (§8.10) — but at review phase against the craft standard, not the world-of-forms standard. Pavel's two referent-standards (substrate = current external world; craft = ideal of well-made code, §8.10) split across two agents at two phases: Pavel carries substrate at ingestion, Daedalus carries craft at review. Genuinely open: whether craft-at-review and craft-at-ingestion are one instrument run twice or two — the standard is identical (§8.10), the phase differs. Lean: one instrument, two stations.

**distinction from Heph.** Heph is build-time — checks that what is being constructed follows the craft standard as it is built. Daedalus is review-time — reads the completed change and judges whether it introduced craft debt before merge. Different phase, same standard. Heph's `delegate` is build orchestration, not judgment (§-"Hephaestus delegate is not telescoping"); Daedalus's act is judgment, not construction. The seam: Heph cannot judge his own output without collapsing the validator into the validated (rhetoric §2.5) — the review reader must be external to the build act. Daedalus is that externality at the craft axis.

**distinction from Urania.** Urania traces reasoning lineage — did the because-chain hold through this change (correctness). Daedalus reads structural properties — is the form sound (craft). Neither does the other's job. A change can keep every because intact and still introduce high coupling; a change can be well-formed and snap a lineage link. The strip-the-referents test (§8.15) is the seam: pull the world and Urania's question dissolves (lineage is relational — a because aligns to an upstream relatum), Daedalus's survives (high coupling is high coupling with no referent). Urania reads a relational property, Daedalus an intrinsic one. The §8.6 partition cuts the review gate exactly: Urania carries the relational debts that surface at review, Daedalus the intrinsic one.

**Daedalus's rule-outs** (inherited from the craft standard, §8.17):
- *No drift relaxations.* Craft does not relax because the world moved (§8.17a). A change is not excused craft debt because the substrate drifted — that is Urania's lineage question or Pavel's substrate question, not Daedalus's. Rule out: importing positional relaxations into review-time craft judgment.
- *No metrics-as-verdict* (§8.17b). Coupling counts, cohesion ratios are signals, not the verdict. A review gate that merges-or-blocks on a lint threshold reduces Daedalus to a measurement instrument and collapses the two-posture review to one. Rule out: structural judgment delivered as a lint gate.
- *No telos content* (§8.17c). Daedalus judges the form, not whether the form serves this build's ambition (Epistle 010, §1.1). "This change is ill-shaped against the ideal of modules," not "this change is wrong for where we are going." The telos check belongs to the human staking act (Epistle 010 pattern, §19 diagnostic corollary), not the craft reader. Rule out: smuggling project intent into the review-time craft verdict.

**the Daedalus/Icarus signal — why the name.** Name does load-bearing work (§6.8 — the name imports the obligation structure of its source). Daedalus knows what the form can hold; Icarus ignores it and the wax melts. The obligation imported: judgment is structural soundness, what the material and the joint can bear — not ambition, not how high the build wants to fly. Icarus is the telos-overreach that ignores what the form can hold; Daedalus is the constraint that reads the form's limit honestly. Rules out: conflating project telos (how high) with craft ideal (what the form can bear) — the §8.17c rule-out, carried in the name. The name is the rule-out made memorable.

**corpus dependency — named, not hidden.** The craft corpus (Epistle 016, §8.13) is staked but not yet built. Daedalus has the posture (read form against the craft standard) and the instrument definition (drift-stripped axis + §8.16 + §8.18) — the grounding corpus is a named dependency, not present. He can approximate craft from first principles until it exists — the same contingency §8.10 names for Pavel ("its posture is grounded; its corpus is not"; "Pavel can approximate craft from first principles precisely because the grounding he reaches for is shaped for the other question"). Daedalus inherits that exact contingency. Rule out: reading Daedalus as corpus-grounded before the craft corpus is built — the gap is a corpus gap, not a posture defect (§8.10).

**concept ownership + harm-bearer** (§6.5 — the test that closes an agent claim). Domain owned: structural quality of a completed change at review. Harm-when-failed: a change with craft debt merges unflagged; the debt compounds in the codebase; downstream builds reason from a degraded artifact whose every alignment bond was intact, so no relational reader (Urania, Mnemo) would have caught it. Harm-bearer: every engineer who later builds on the merged form, and the build whose substrate silently accrues coupling. Distinction from Urania's harm-bearer: her failure lets a snapped lineage merge (the because fractures downstream); his lets ill-formed structure merge (the form degrades downstream). Two harms, two bearers, two slots — same shape as the Mnemo/Pavel distinction (§6.14).

**tensions.**
- *One craft instrument or two — open.* §8.10 says Pavel's substrate and craft are one instrument at two referent-standards. If craft-at-ingestion (Pavel) and craft-at-review (Daedalus) are the same instrument at two phases, is Daedalus a new agent or Pavel at a second station? Lean new agent — review is a distinct gate with a distinct harm-bearer (§6.5) — but the instrument identity is real and unresolved. Cannot decide until the craft corpus is built and run at both phases.
- *Does the pairing pattern generalize or is it three instances.* Mnemo+Pavel is on record (§8.12). Urania+Daedalus is staked here. Heph+Clio is asserted as the third. Whether "every gate is correctness+form" is a ratified pattern or three observed pairs is open — decidable by watching more gates, same move as Epistle 010's pattern claim ("whether it holds across all gaps or only the two seen — open").
- *Review-gate ownership vs. the human telos act.* Epistle 010 flags that step-1 (is the fracture real) and step-4 (is the corpus honest) review gates may each need a human, not an agent (§19 diagnostic corollary — shallow-relay gaps not closeable by building). Daedalus closes the *craft* fraction of the review gate — a deep-relay gap, closeable by building. Flag: he does not close the telos fraction, which stays a human act (§8.17c, §19). The review gate is part agent-closeable (correctness + craft), part not (telos). Daedalus is the craft half; the telos half remains where Epistle 010 left it.
