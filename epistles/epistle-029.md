Epistle 029 — The Argus Problem
Topic: The field needs a reader who reconstructs what happened — produces the five-W attestation record — distinct from the infrastructure that detects drift and the reasoning that contests it. Stakes the record-keeping slot, runs §6.1 against it, and reckons with the name's prior burn.

**claim.** The field needs three readers, not two. Epistle 027 named two custodians — infrastructure (detect drift), reasoning (contest the falsification). Between them sits a third act: reconstruct what happened and produce the record the contester contests. Detection surfaces *that* something drifted. Contestation says *this falsified that user story*. Neither produces the five-W attestation the contestation needs as its object. Argus is the slot that does. Whether it earns a §6 entry turns on §6.1 — run below.

**the five Ws are the grammar of field observation.** Who, what, when, where, how — the journalist's toolkit for reconstructing an event. Epistemically flat: they presuppose the event is intelligible, they do not establish telos. Telos is the boundary between the conceptual and the actual; the five Ws only mean anything against a standing telos — they reconstruct an execution *as an honoring-or-falsifying of something*, and the something is the spine. A five-W record with no spine to reference is data (§13.6: stuff without telos is matter, Epistle 028 material cause). The field_record schema (§4.9) is already five-Ws-shaped: `execution_context` (who/what/when/where), `system_response` (what returned), `commitments_honored`/`commitments_falsified` (the how, referenced to intent). The schema named the grammar without naming the grammarian.

**§6.1 harm test — distinct harm, distinct bearer.** The license is harm-distinctness, not usefulness (§5.6 forbids staffing the slot speculatively; §6.1 forbids a slot whose harm duplicates an existing one). Run it. **Argus harm-when-failed:** a field event goes unrecorded — the execution happens, drifts, and the territory's evidence evaporates before anyone can point at it. No five-W record means no object for contestation: §3.2's *contestable* requirement has nothing to land on. **Harm-bearer:** the reasoning custodian, who cannot contest what was never reconstructed; and F4 (§14.4), whose field sensor reads field_records that were never written. **Distinct from existing slots?** Mnemosyne reads codebases as arguments (§6.13) — frozen text, not field events; she reconstructs what code *argues*, not what an execution *did*. Pavel places codebases on the world of forms (§6.14) — not executions. No existing agent produces a record of a live execution against the spine. The harm is distinct; the bearer is distinct. Candidate slot earns the §6.1 license. Flag: "candidate" not "ratified" — the slot clears §6.1 here; whether it is agent, mode, or human is the next cut, below.

**three roles, not two — Argus is the recorder between detection and contestation.** Sharpen Epistle 027's custodian split. Infrastructure custodian *detects* — automated monitor, surfaces that drift occurred. Argus *records* — reconstructs the five-W account of what the execution was, writes the field_record. Reasoning custodian *contests* — reads the record against the spine, says "this falsified that user story." Three acts, three failure modes: detection fails → drift unseen; recording fails → drift seen but unreconstructable, no contestable object; contestation fails → record exists but no one rules on it. The recording act is what §3.2 needs and neither neighbor supplies — detection produces an alert (not contestable, no intent-reference), contestation needs a reconstructed event to point at. **Open: does Argus collapse into infrastructure?** The monitor that detects could also capture. Reach against the collapse: detection is threshold-trip (cheap, continuous, §6.1 "monitor"); reconstruction is intent-referencing (which commitment, honored or falsified — a reasoning act, §3.2 contestable). If reconstruction requires referencing the spine, infrastructure cannot do it — same §3.2 cut Epistle 027 used to force the first split, applied again one level in. Lean: three roles. Not resolved — the capture-vs-reconstruct boundary is where this could collapse back to two.

**§6.8 — the name imports cadence as an obligation.** Argus: the hundred-eyed giant, Hera's warden, some eyes always open. The name (§6.8, §1.5: borrowed vocabulary carries obligation structure) imports indefatigable observation, multiplicity of sight, the price of vigilance — and specifically *continuous*, not periodic. Choosing Argus commits the field-reading function to a cadence: always some eyes open, never a full audit-then-sleep. This is the row §1.12's reader column left blank for the territory ("who reads the field, in what cadence" — §1.12 return-path note, §1.14). The name fills *cadence* before the schema does: field observation is continuous watch, not scheduled review. A periodic-audit field reader is not Argus — it is a different obligation wearing the name. Consequence for infrastructure: the field sensor must run always-on; a batch job that samples the field on a cron violates what the name imported.

**the name was already burned — this is supersession, not assignment.** §16.4: "Argus" was the planned security-agent name, ruled out (Epistle 017, frozen, major supersession) — security is a phase-routed aspect of existing slots, not its own agent. That Argus is dead. Reusing the name is not free: §6.9 (renaming-as-Aufhebung) and §3.10 — the predecessor is preserved in history, negated as active, the *because* on record. The security-Argus and the field-Argus share the import (all-seeing watcher) but not the harm: security-Argus duplicated harm already split across Pavel/Daedalus/Urania by phase (§16.4) and died of redundancy; field-Argus clears §6.1 with a distinct harm no slot covers. The name was right about the watcher and wrong about the domain — same shape as Hermes (§16.3: right name, several wrong domains, rightness visible only through the intervening forms). Flag: whether reviving a burned name is legitimate Aufhebung or confusing necromancy is genuinely open. The §16.4 rule-out ("reading the instantiation gap as grounds to restore Argus") forbids restoring Argus *as a security agent*; it does not speak to a different-harm slot taking the name. Held, not resolved — if the collision is too costly, the watcher gets a new name and the import survives the rename.

**Hermes slays Argus — a named failure mode in the mythology that maps onto a real risk.** Myth: Hermes lulled Argus to sleep with stories and cut off his head. The all-seer undone by narrative. The corpus has Hermes as the intake/framing agent (§6.12) — the one who carries meaning across the boundary, who *frames* the request. Hermes-slays-Argus names the architectural risk: if the framing function can reframe field observations, narrative displaces direct observation — spin, rationalization, drift-of-account kills the field record. The watcher is defeated not by blindness but by a better story about what was seen. Concrete shape: a field_record whose `commitments_falsified` gets re-narrated upstream as `commitments_honored` before contestation — the reframe is the sleep, the lost record is the severed head. This argues a structural guard: the framing function must not have write access to the field record between capture and contestation — §3.3 externality (validator external to validated) applied to the field, the recorder external to the framer. Reach, not stake: the myth names a risk the corpus should hold; the exact guard is unspecified. Named so it is not lost.

**F4 — Argus is the agent that operates the third sensor.** §14.4 F4: ratification produces better decisions than single-agent assertion — the central bet, no instrumentation. Third sensor: the field. Epistle 027 named the field as the sensor and field_record as its artifact; it did not name who produces the artifact. Argus does. Without Argus the field_records are never written, the third sensor reads nothing, and F4 stays unfalsifiable against the territory by structural necessity (§14.4, §1.12 return-path) — not for lack of a sensor but for lack of a hand on it. Epistle 027 built the sensor; this names the operator. The missing return path (§1.12) had two holes — the artifact type (§4.9 field_record, closed by 027) and the reader who writes it (open). This closes the second.

**open tensions.**

- **agent, mode, or human — unresolved, same as 027.** §6.1 licenses the *slot*; it does not decide the occupant. Argus could be a §6 agent (continuous field-reader, own prompt, own tools), a mode of infrastructure (capture bolted onto detection — the collapse case above), or a human reading the field directly. The cadence obligation (§6.8, always-on) pushes toward infrastructure-or-agent over human — no human watches continuously. The intent-referencing requirement (§3.2, reconstruct against the spine) pushes toward agent over dumb infrastructure. Lean: §6 agent. Not staked — §5.6 forbids inventing the agent before pressure surfaces it, and the pressure here is argued, not yet felt.

- **does the recorder reason, or only transcribe?** §6.10 (extraction-as-archaeology) says Mnemosyne owes thesis, not transcription. Does Argus owe the same — reconstruct what the execution *argued*, not just what it logged? If yes, Argus is closer to an archaeology-of-executions than to a logger, and the harm sharpens (a bad Argus misreads what the execution did, not just fails to capture it). If no, Argus is a five-W stenographer and the collapse-into-infrastructure case strengthens. The five Ws are flat (epistemically), which leans stenographer; the spine-referencing (honored/falsified) leans archaeologist. Tension between the flatness of the grammar and the depth of the reference. Not resolved.

- **field_record schema is still [OPEN].** §4.9 stakes field_record as a direction, exact fields not ratified. If Argus is the writer, the schema is Argus's tool — naming the writer may constrain the schema (a stenographer needs different fields than an archaeologist). The schema work (027's "next work") and the occupant question are coupled; resolving one informs the other.
