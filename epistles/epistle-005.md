Epistle 005 — What Is a Corpus?
Topic: A corpus is not artifacts-with-a-tag. It is a bounded domain plus a retrieval surface plus a write discipline plus a frozen core — and the relationship of an agent to it is structural, not nominal.

§6.6 names corpora. Does not define one. Gap has teeth: shitcorpus (Epistle 001) wants the name; ur-software corpus (Pavel) and anima corpus (corpus_logic, corpus_rhetoric, praxis) already coexist and already share vocabulary. Without a definition, "corpus" is a label on a folder. Per §3.16, a structurally compliant label with hollow content is indistinguishable from the real thing until something breaks.

Stake: a corpus has five parts. All five required.

1. Bounded domain. Named scope. Sharp edge. Ur-software domain = positional diagnosis on the world of forms (CAP, fallacies, supersession spine). Anima domain = how domain knowledge propagates through this system. Vocabulary overlap (agent, artifact, ratification) is not domain overlap. Domain is what the corpus answers questions about, not what words it uses. Sharpness is structural work — naming the harm-bearer per §6.5. If two corpora claim the same harm-when-failed, one of them is wrong.

2. Concept ownership. Named agent. Harm-when-failed per §6.5. Pavel owns ur-software because Pavel takes the hit when positional diagnosis is wrong. Hermes/Mnemosyne/Clio own the anima corpus through their reasoning surface. Open: shitcorpus has no agent who reasons FROM it. Future prospectors reason AGAINST it. Is that ownership or is that something else? Don't know yet. Possibly the relationship-to-corpus is itself typed (reasons_from vs reasons_against) and ownership obtains under either. Flag.

3. Retrieval surface. Not tag-matching. Typed-edge traversal per §14. A corpus you can only query by corpus_id == X is a folder. A corpus has internal structure — supersession chains, citation graphs, elaboration trees — and retrieval honors that structure. F1 (graph > flat on lineage queries) is the load-bearing claim here. If F1 fails empirically, this leg of the definition needs revision; right now pilot supports it.

4. Write discipline. Who writes, under what citation requirements, with what ratification cycle. Anima corpus: corpus_logic is §18 conviction prose under ratification; corpus_rhetoric is §4 doxa moves. Discipline is per-corpus, non-negotiable per corpus, named. Shitcorpus discipline = write at discard time, citation to the discard event, no retroactive entries. Ur-software discipline = TBD (Pavel queued). A corpus without write discipline accumulates contamination by default. Mechanism: absent a named discipline, any artifact adjacent to the domain can enter under the corpus's tag without citation scrutiny — the corpus becomes a sink, and downstream agents reason from sediment they cannot distinguish from stake.

5. Frozen core. At least some artifacts staked as reasoned-from. Frozen ⊥ confidence (per Anima vocab). The core is what an agent reasons from when it has nothing else; it phase-changes to revise. A "corpus" with no frozen core is liquid storage with a domain tag — useful, but not a corpus in the §6.3/§6a sense.

Contamination diagnostic: if two corpora share a frozen artifact, one of them is borrowing what it should be citing. Cross-corpus citation is fine and expected. Cross-corpus freezing is the contamination signature.

→ tensions
- Is "reasoned against" (shitcorpus) sufficient agent-relationship, or does corpus require reasons_from? Genuinely open. The five-part definition admits both readings.
- Retrieval surface depends on F1; F1 not yet falsified at K=30–50. If graph traversal doesn't beat flat lexical at significance, leg 3 weakens to "tag-matching plus something" — undefined.
- Write discipline and prompt governance (§15.2/§15.8) look like the same shape. They aren't. Write discipline can go structural now: store the discipline as a frozen artifact inside the corpus, ratifiable through the normal cycle. §3.3 doesn't block — the discipline artifact is external to any agent's execution, so other agents and humans contest and supersede without self-ratification. Two surfaces separate cleanly: what enters the corpus (write discipline — solvable via frozen artifact) vs. how agents reason from it (prompt-encoded retrieval behavior — still blocked on §15.8/§15.9). This epistle closes the first. Second stays open.
- §6.6 needs revision to carry this definition. This epistle is vapor; excavation pending.
