Epistle 035 — The Shitcorping Loop Has a Felt Terminus, Not a Scheduled One
Topic: process.md's Step 1→2→3 describes the loop's governance, not its epistemic shape. The real shape is iterative — pan/smelt/pan/smelt — and where it stops is felt, not scheduled.

process.md holds the shitcorping loop as three steps: lite proposes (Step 1) → author responds (Step 2) → builder commits (Step 3). True as written. Names who acts, when the record is written, who owns each move. That is governance — and the governance is correct.

What it undersells: the *shape of the reasoning* inside the loop. Step 1→2→3 reads as a forward pass. Pan once, respond once, commit. As if the loop knew its destination before it started.

It doesn't. The actual shape is iterative. Pan, smelt, pan again, smelt again — where the next destination may be re-panning the same material the loop already touched. The material teaches the panner what to look for next. The gold is not visible until the panning reveals it; the panning does not know what it is reaching for until it has reached. This is the directed shape of reasoning (§11.4): the smelter does not know the telos in advance, stays in the conversation long enough to find it.

Each pass is a micro-Aufhebung (§3.10). What survives the pan is elevated; what doesn't is gravel; the next pass begins from what survived. Not erasure of the rejected — it stays on the record as what was tried. The loop is a supersession cycle at the scale of a single sitting — the same shape as the ratification cycle itself (§7.1): freeze what holds, re-pan what doesn't.

Why this matters, operationally: the panner needs to know the loop may circle back. A panner who reads Step 1→2→3 as a single forward pass stops too early. Calls gravel what hasn't been panned enough to reveal what it's reaching for. The premature gravel call is the failure mode — not because the call is wrong, but because the material was never panned to the point where the call could be made. Single-pass panning is structurally inadequate to material that teaches.

The receipt: this loop's own provenance. Epistle 033's shitcorping. Raw notes → lite pans the conversation → author pushes back on a gravel call → re-pan with the pushback as new context → the load-bearing cut emerges. Lite had called Ari-as-tutor gravel. Author pushed: gravel for Hermes, not for Ari. The pushback forced a re-pan of the slot/face distinction — and the slot/face argument that came out of that re-pan was the cut that shaped the whole epistle. It was not in the raw material. It was not in lite's first pass. The panning found it, on the second turn, *because* the first verdict was contested and the material got worked again. Drop the re-pan and the epistle is poorer — the load-bearing claim never surfaces. The loop's value was in its circling back, exactly what the linear reading omits.

Scope: the iterative shape applies to both loops process.md names — the drafting/revision loop (a) and the shitcorping-for-ingestion loop (b). Sharpest for (b): the pan against the live corpus is where "what is this reaching for" is the whole question, and the answer is found by working the material, not declared at the top.

Rules out: treating Step 1→2→3 as an exhaustive description of the loop's shape (it is exhaustive of the governance, silent on the epistemics); treating a single-pass pan as adequate; calling gravel before the material has been worked enough to reveal what it reaches for. The amendment does not contradict the steps — it names the shape the steps move through. Governance is linear; reasoning is iterative; both are true.

Open — the terminus is felt, not protocol. The loop has no natural stopping condition short of the author accepting. "Comes out just right" is a judgment, not a checkpoint. When has the material been panned *enough*? Pan too few times and you call premature gravel; pan forever and nothing freezes. The felt terminus is the craft (§11.6 — calibration of when to cut and when to fill, practiced instinct not procedure) — and like all craft it resists being written down. Author-acceptance is the governance stop, but it is not the epistemic one: the author can accept too early too. This epistle names the shape and does not close the stopping question. Genuinely open: whether a felt terminus can be made legible without being falsified into a protocol.
