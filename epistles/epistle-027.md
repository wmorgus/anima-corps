Epistle 027 — The Field
Topic: The corpus models intent→territory in full and territory→intent almost not at all. The field is where the running system answers back. Names the missing feedback half: the asymmetry §1.12 owns but never returns to.

**claim.** Intent→territory is richly modeled; territory→intent is nearly silent. The corpus has the whole forward model — spine, hinge, ratification (§3), the artifact taxonomy (§1.12), every register type. It has almost no model for the running system feeding back into intent. The field is where the territory answers back, and the corpus is deaf to that answer. This asymmetry is the gap.

**§1.12 brackets the territory and never returns.** "Running system — the referent, not a register. The territory the spine claims about." Named, acknowledged, immediately left. The territory is the moment of truth — where every upstream commitment is honored or falsified — and the corpus has no model for what happens when that moment speaks. §1.12 owns the asymmetry ("the asymmetry is owned, not papered over") and then papers over the return path by treating the territory as terminal. The referent talks back. The corpus has no ear.

**the field is already a concept, structurally missing as infrastructure.** §1.6: "code in production is a frozen suggestion, not truth. A bug is a falsified suggestion, superseded by a better-informed one." A falsified suggestion is a field event. It has no artifact type. No path back to the dojo, no path back to the forge (Epistle 026). The supersession is *assumed* — §1.6 says superseded, never says by what mechanism, traveling which register, read by whom. Concept present, infrastructure absent. The §1.12 reader column gap, applied to the territory: who reads the field, in what cadence, to detect falsification — unspecified.

**two consumers, two needs, split by material.** The field feeds two surfaces that need different things from it — same forge/dojo cut as Epistle 026, now on the return path.
- **DOJO need — aggregated patterns, conviction testing.** Are the beliefs holding in reality? Does the field confirm or falsify what was staked? This feeds §4.9's empirical_record path: field data as the sensor for the unstaked F-claims in §14.
- **FORGE need — specific instances, point-in-time snapshots. God mode.** What did the actual execution look like at this place and time? Not the spec, not the code — the execution itself, full fidelity. One instance, not an aggregate.
The cut is material, not stage: the dojo reads the field as evidence about convictions; the forge reads it as a captured instance to reason from. Aggregate vs. instance. Two readers, two needs.

**god mode = intent-referenced observability.** Calliope holds god-mode context for dojo reasoning — full ratification trail, every artifact, the complete because-chain (§4). The forge needs the field equivalent: a snapshot of what the software actually *was* at a specific execution — which user, what intent registered, what the system returned, whether the behavior honored the upstream commitment (user story, AC, §1.12 hinge). This is intent-referenced observability, not performance observability. Standard observability asks "is the system up and fast?" Intent-referenced observability asks "is the system honoring what it committed to?" The distinction is the whole claim. A field god-mode snapshot makes a specific execution *contestable* — point at it, say "this falsified that user story." That contestability is §3.2's requirement (recorded, contestable, structurally external) applied to the field. Performance observability has no §3.2 face; intent-referenced observability is built for it.

**field observations may be a register, not just evidence from the referent.** §1.12 calls the running system "the referent, not a register." A field observation — a specific execution captured with §4.4 field fidelity — is structured evidence shaped from the territory. If it can be ratified and cited back into the spine, it functions as a register. Tension with the live §1.12 claim, not a resolution: the running system *as territory* stays the referent; but a field-observation artifact, *shaped from* that territory, could be a register. The corpus claim "the running system is the referent, not a register" may be half wrong — true of the territory, false of the artifact drawn from it. Held open. The territory is not a register; what you capture of it might be.

**F4 has no field sensor — the field is where the central bet would be tested.** §14.4 F4: ratification produces better decisions than single-agent assertion. The corpus's central claim. Zero empirical instrumentation. The field is exactly where you instrument it — do systems built with full ratification discipline produce better *field* outcomes than those without? The field god-mode is the sensor. Without field infrastructure, F4 cannot be tested, and "this is the central claim of the architecture and it has no falsification protocol" (§14.4) stays true by structural necessity, not by oversight. The missing return path is why the central bet is unfalsifiable.

**what remains open.**
- **T1 — field observation: new artifact type or empirical_record?** §4.9's empirical_record requires a `measurement_protocol`. A field observation at a specific execution is not an experiment — it is a capture. It may need a distinct type — `execution_snapshot`, `field_record` — or it may fit empirical_record with field-capture as a protocol variant. A capture is not a measurement; forcing it under empirical_record may launder a snapshot as an experiment. Not resolved. Bears on T1 of Epistle 026's substrate question — what crosses the boundary, in which type.
- **T2 — who reads the field and routes it back?** The dojo path (aggregate → empirical_record → conviction testing) has no current agent. The forge path (execution snapshot → god mode) has no current agent. Whether these are agent slots, modes of existing agents (§6.2), or infrastructure with no named carrier is open. §5.6 forbids inventing agents for imagined gaps — let pressure surface them. So this is named as pressure, not staffed as a slot. Flag: the field may be the first reasoning surface whose reader is infrastructure, not agent — and if so, §1.12's reader column has a row the §6 constellation cannot fill.
