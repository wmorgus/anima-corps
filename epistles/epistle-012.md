Epistle 012 — The Vow in the Name
Topic: "Shitcorp" was chosen for its discomfort. The discomfort is load-bearing — sacred in the profane. The naming is the vow: willingness to say the word is the commitment signal that the work of keeping accidental error out is taken seriously.

**claim.** The word "shitcorp" earns its place by being uncomfortable to say. Not despite the discomfort — because of it. The discomfort is the function, not a cost paid for memorability. A clean term (curate, filter, refine) would name the same act and carry no vow. "Shitcorp" makes the speaker stake something each time they use it.

**the shit is accidental, not essential.** Name the referent precisely. Shit = the contingent garbage that has no right to be in the work: silly details, small errors, sloppy provenance, unverified claims, the uncited discard (§3.20). Not the hard problems — those are essential, the conviction the architecture exists to carry (§1.11, Epistle 011 shallow-relay). The shit is what could have been kept out and wasn't. The kind of thing that sinks a research study not because the thesis was wrong but because a footnote was. Accidental in the strict sense: removable without touching what the work is.

**keeping shit out is how Anima earns teleological status.** Teleological status = software with a purpose it is faithful to. Faithfulness is not a property you declare — it is the residual after the accidental is removed. A system carrying unverified claims and silent filters (§3.20 failure mode) is not faithful to its telos; it is faithful to whatever noise leaked in. The shitcorp act — record the discard, cite the because, drop the candidate (§4.9, §6.6) — is the mechanism by which the system stays pointed at what it committed to. F4 makes this measurable: declining egregious-error rate is keeping-shit-out, instrumented (§14.4 F4). Earn the telos by subtraction.

**the naming is the vow.** A vow is a commitment made costly so the commitment is legible. "Shitcorp" imports obligation structure the way borrowed vocabulary always does (rhetoric §1.12) — but here the import is deliberate and the obligation is to oneself. Willing to say the ugly word → serious about the ugly work. The discomfort filters: anyone who reaches for a softer term is signaling they want the act without the vow. The name is a tell (rhetoric §1.12) that the speaker has taken the obligation. This extends §3.8's logic — when the telos is right the name arrives uninvited (rhetoric §1.12, §3.8). Here the telos (ruthless removal of the accidental) summoned a name that refuses to be comfortable, because comfort would launder the seriousness the act requires.

**sacred in the profane.** The vow is sacred — a binding commitment to faithfulness. The word is profane — deliberately low, ugly, unserious-sounding. The two are not in tension; the profanity is the carrier of the sacred. A solemn word for a solemn act invites performed solemnity (the inflation §3.8 rules out elsewhere — confidence laundered by register). A profane word for a sacred act cannot be performed. You cannot say "shitcorp" reverentially. The word strips the performance and leaves only the act. Holiness that refuses ornament.

**tensions.**

- *Does the vow survive familiarity — genuinely open.* A word's discomfort decays with use. If "shitcorp" becomes ordinary in the mouths of the people who say it daily, does the commitment signal degrade to zero, leaving only an ugly synonym for filtering? Flag. Possible the vow has to be renewed by something other than the word — or possible the residual ugliness is permanent and never fully domesticates. Undetermined.

- *Is the discomfort a filter or a barrier — held, not resolved.* Same property that screens out the uncommitted may screen out the committed-but-decorous: a serious worker who will not adopt a crude register. If so the name selects for a temperament, not a commitment, and the two are being conflated. Cannot tell from here whether discomfort tracks seriousness or merely tracks tolerance-for-crudeness.
