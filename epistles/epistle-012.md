Epistle 012 — Three Debts, One Partition
Topic: Are epistemic, substrate, and spec debt (§8.1, §8.2, §8.4) a complete taxonomy of software problem types? Stake the partition and its principle, and turn the three debts into explicit search buckets for extraction rather than undifferentiated "problems."

**claim.** The three debts are not three arbitrary buckets. One partition. The principle: a software problem is a broken because-link, and there are exactly three links that can break. (1) Intent → code: the code no longer implements the live intent — spec debt (§8.1). (2) Artifact → because: the because was never captured or is lost — epistemic debt (§8.2). (3) Code → world: internal chain intact, external substrate drifted past it — substrate debt (§8.4). Three links because the architecture binds three things: a stated intent, a recorded because, an external referent. A problem is one of these bonds failing. Extends §8 — names the partition principle §8 implies but does not state.

**the partition test.** For any software problem, ask: which because-link broke. The answer is forced and singular per link. Spec debt — is the code coherent against its origin but the origin moved (§8.1)? Epistemic debt — is the because absent, every change unquantifiable risk (§8.2)? Substrate debt — is the internal chain intact but the world it was coherent against gone (§8.4)? The three correctives confirm the cut: supersession addresses spec (§3.10), citation addresses epistemic (§3.11), positional diagnosis addresses substrate (§6.14) — three disciplines, three debts, no discipline serving two debts, no debt unserved. The corrective map is the receipt that the partition is real, not a list.

**exhaustive over Mnemo's detection surface, not over all software.** Claim bounded — but bound it precisely. The three debts are exhaustive over what Mnemo can detect from the artifact chain: broken because-links, each readable by archaeology. Not a claim about all software pathology. The gap is craft debt — badly structured code: high coupling, low cohesion, tangled deps, accumulated accidental complexity. Craft debt creates maintenance burden with no broken because-link. The intent→code, artifact→because, code→world bonds are all intact; the artifact is merely poorly formed. The taxonomy does not generate craft debt because no link snapped. And Mnemo cannot read craft debt from the chain alone — structural quality is not a fracture in the argument, it is the shape of the argument's prose. Craft debt is real and excluded. State the bound this way or the completeness claim overreaches.

**the partition falls onto two postures.** The completeness claim is not "all software debt" — it is "all debt legible from each posture." The cut: spec debt and epistemic debt live inside the artifact chain → archaeology finds them → Mnemo's domain (§6.13). Substrate debt lives at the code→world boundary → diagnosis finds it → Pavel's domain (§6.14). Craft debt is also Pavel's, not Mnemo's — it is the analog of substrate debt on the diagnosis side: a quality of the form, read by judging the form, not by tracing a snapped link. So the three because-link debts are exactly the debts Mnemo can detect, and craft debt is the part of Pavel's surface that the link-taxonomy does not touch. Completeness is posture-relative. Each posture sees the debts its instrument can read; no posture sees all debt.

**the fourth thing §8.5 names is not a fourth debt.** §8.5 names a thin artifact chain — agents cannot reason from an underdeveloped record — as a runtime fault alongside spec and epistemic debt. Resist reading this as debt-type four. A thin chain is not a broken bond; it is too few bonds laid down. Epistemic debt is a because that should exist and is absent (link broke). A thin chain is a because-network never built dense enough to carry. Adjacent, distinct: epistemic debt is a hole in the record, thin chain is a record too sparse to traverse. Both surface as the inverted review/rescue ratio (§8.5), which is why §8.5 lists them together — same signal, different fault. Flag: the line between "missing because" and "too few becauses" may not be crisp at the margin. Held, not resolved.

**Mnemo searches three buckets, not one.** Payoff. The partition converts extraction from "find the problems" into three typed searches. Mnemosyne (§6.13) does archaeology — surfaces what the code argues and where the argument fractured. The fracture is not generic. It is one of three: the code argues a stale intent (spec), the code argues with no recoverable because (epistemic), the code argues against a world that moved (substrate, the §6.14 Pavel boundary). Undifferentiated "problem" hunting under-specifies the search; the agent does not know what shape of fracture it is looking for. Three buckets make the obligation explicit: for each fracture, which because-link broke. This sharpens §6.13's reasoning obligation ("name where the argument fractured without being named as a fracture") — fracture has a type, and the type is the debt.

**the archaeology/diagnosis split falls out of the partition.** §8.3 splits Mnemosyne (archaeology) from Pavel (diagnosis) and §6.13/§6.14 give them different harms. The partition explains the split mechanically. Spec and epistemic debt are internal — they live in the intent→code and artifact→because links, both inside the codebase's own chain. Archaeology reads the internal chain (Mnemosyne). Substrate debt is external — the code→world link — and reading it requires the world of forms, not the codebase alone. Diagnosis reads the external link (Pavel, §6.14, OPEN). The two postures are not two styles; they are the internal-link buckets versus the external-link bucket. §8.3's "diagnosis is not archaeology" is the partition's internal/external seam. Craft debt sits on Pavel's side of this seam too — not a broken external link but a quality of the form, read by judgment, outside the link-taxonomy entirely (see above).

**tensions.**

- *Are the three links orthogonal or ordered — genuinely open.* The partition assumes a clean cut: a problem is exactly one broken link. Real codebases carry compound debt — stale intent (spec) whose because was also never recorded (epistemic). Is that two debts coincident, or one root with two faces? If links can break in causal sequence — missing because → drift unnoticed → spec rot — then "which link broke" has an order, and the singular-answer test (above) is a snapshot that loses the chain. Flag. The buckets survive as search targets either way; the *independence* of the buckets may not.

- *Whether the detection surface is stable.* The bound (exhaustive over Mnemo's detection surface, not over all software) holds only if the surface is fixed. If the architecture later binds a fourth thing — say, a performance contract as a first-class referent — a fourth because-link appears, a fourth debt-type, and the partition was never complete, only complete-so-far. Don't know whether the three bonds are the architecture's permanent commitment or its current one. Bounds the exhaustiveness claim to the corpus as it stands.

- *Whether verification debt is epistemic debt or a fourth category — genuinely open.* Test debt / verification debt: no safe way to reason about whether a change is correct. Read one way, this is a subspecies of epistemic debt — the because that lets you reason safely about a change is absent, so every change is unquantifiable risk (§8.2), and missing tests are just one form of that missing because. Read another way, it is distinct: epistemic debt is about why the artifact is what it is; verification debt is about whether a proposed change preserves correctness — a forward-looking guarantee, not a recovered history. The two may not collapse. If distinct, it is a fourth because-link inside Mnemo's surface and the partition is three-plus-one, not three. Unresolved. Held.
