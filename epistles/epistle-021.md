Epistle 021 — The composition-discipline file
Topic: §1.15 ratified the merge condition for logic.md §18 and rhetoric.md §4. This stakes the merge target — what the file holds, what it does not disturb, where it lives, what becomes of §18 and rhetoric §4 after.

§1.15 ruled the merge. One question — "how must I compose into a corpus?" — two homes, same reader (corpus author), same rate of change. Merge condition met. §1.15 named the target abstractly: "one composition-discipline file." This epistle stakes the concrete thing. Does not perform the migration (§7).

**1. The question. Fourth register.** The file answers "how must I write into a corpus?" — authoring discipline. Not conviction (logic.md: what the corpus commits to). Not doxa (rhetoric.md: how truth lands in conversation). Not glossary (§1.13, Epistle 019: what a term means). A fourth register, addressed to the corpus author at the moment of writing an entry. The other three are read FROM while reasoning; this one is read BEFORE writing. Different reader-moment → different file. This is the §1.13 reader/rate test, applied to the act of authoring rather than the content authored.

**2. What it inherits.** Two streams, both authoring discipline:

From logic.md §18 — the within-corpus composition rule. Conviction vs. empirical vs. deliberative registers (§18.1–18.4). Prohibited vocabulary per register (no *might* in conviction; no "this proves" in empirical). Paragraph structure (claim → consequence → what it forbids). The seam rule between conviction and empirical prose (§18.3 — cite the empirical_record, do not summarize inline). Mixed-register-as-defect (§18.6). §17 sui-generis exemption (§18.5). All of it: how a §-claim is written.

From rhetoric.md §4 — the cross-corpus composition rule. The three entry types: `move` / `tell` / `defense` (§4.1), tag-by-dominant-type, note load-bearing mixes. The discipline of writing an entry that earns its place by being deployable, not staked. The instruction half of the seam — "when writing into either corpus, ask: staked conviction or deployable move? If both, split" (§4.2 final paragraph).

Both streams answer the same question at different scope: §18 disciplines composition *within* the logos corpus, rhetoric §4 disciplines composition *across* the seam. §4.2 already names the pairing — "this rule pairs with corpus_logic §18." The merge collapses the pairing into one file. Don't know yet: whether the within/across distinction survives as section structure inside the merged file or dissolves into one discipline. Flag for the migration.

**3. What it does NOT disturb. The seam itself stays.** Critical cut. Rhetoric §4.2 carries two things, and they separate:

(a) The seam *claim* — logos lives in logic.md, doxa lives in rhetoric.md, neither holds the other's entries, cite across. This is a claim ABOUT the two corpora — a routing rule for where an entry belongs, and the because (laundered logos loses staked weight; laundered doxa acquires false authority). It is conviction-shaped. It does not move.

(b) The seam *discipline* — the authoring check ("ask: staked or deployable? if both, split"). This is how to write against the seam. It moves.

The merge takes (b), leaves (a). The logos/doxa boundary is not authoring discipline; it is architecture about what the two corpora are. Moving the discipline out leaves the seam intact and in place. Stake: the seam claim probably lands as a short pointer live in both logic.md and rhetoric.md (each corpus declares its own boundary), or as a §-claim in logic.md cited from rhetoric.md. Genuinely open which — that is a placement question for the seam-as-conviction, separable from this merge. Flag, do not resolve here.

Same cut applies to §18.3's within-corpus seam rule (conviction↔empirical): that is authoring discipline → it moves with §18.

**4. Where it lives.** `corpora/anima/composition.md`. Because: it governs writing into *Anima's* corpora — the register vocabulary, the three entry types, the conviction/empirical/deliberative split are Anima's structure, not universal. Inside `corpora/anima/` puts it beside logic.md, rhetoric.md, glossary.md — the four files an Anima corpus author touches, three read-from and one read-before. Not a shared file across `corpora/ur-software/*`: those corpora have simpler writing conventions and do not carry the conviction/empirical register machinery. A shared composition file would either over-constrain ur-software or dilute to uselessness. If ur-software later needs authoring discipline, that is its own file. Rules out: a repo-root `composition.md` governing all corpora uniformly.

**5. What becomes of §18 and rhetoric §4.** Asymmetric, because the two sources are not the same shape.

§18 — a numbered section in a §-numbered corpus. Removing it outright either renumbers §§19+ (breaks every cross-reference) or leaves a hole. Cleaner: §18 becomes a stub pointer. "§18 Rendering registers — migrated to composition.md. [→ composition.md]." The number is preserved, the content relocates, inbound citations (§18.3, §18.4, §18.6 are referenced) still resolve to the stub which redirects. The stub is cheap and the renumber is expensive — take the stub.

Rhetoric §4 — splits at the cut from §3. The authoring discipline (entry types §4.1, the seam *check* in §4.2's final paragraph) migrates to composition.md. The seam *claim* (§4.2's logos/doxa routing rule + because) stays as the boundary declaration — either in place in rhetoric §4 reduced to the claim alone, or relocated to the seam's chosen home (open, per §3). So rhetoric §4 does not become a pure stub: part moves, part stays. The flagged review comment in §4.2 (the §2.21/§2.22/§2.13 drift-test for Will) is seam-claim work, not authoring discipline — it stays with the seam.

**6. Scope.** This epistle stakes the file: its question, its contents, its location, the cut between what moves and what stays, the disposition of both sources. It does NOT perform the migration. Pulling §18 content and rhetoric §4 authoring-discipline into composition.md, writing the §18 stub, splitting rhetoric §4, placing the seam claim — later work, downstream of this epistle earning weight. The hardest open piece handed forward: where the seam-as-conviction lives (§3). That placement is separable and should not block the merge.
