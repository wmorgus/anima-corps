Epistle 020 — The severity register
Topic: logic.md records what Anima commits to and what each commitment rules out. It does not record how hard each commitment was tested or what survived. That is a second question for a second reader at a second rate of change. The split condition (019) is met. Stake the severity register as the document that should exist but does not.

The claim. A severity register should exist alongside logic.md: a cross-cutting record answering "what was tried against this §-claim, and what survived?" Not the conviction. Not which claims are bet. The record of test severity — what scrutiny each claim took and lived through.

The split condition, applied (019). logic.md answers "what does Anima commit to, and what does that rule out?" — conviction, falsified by the world, written when a conviction is ratified, read by anyone reasoning FROM the corpus. The severity register answers "how hard was this tested, and what survived?" — provenance of scrutiny, written when a test is run, updated when tests are added (not only when the corpus changes), read by whoever needs to know whether a claim is load-bearing-because-survived or load-bearing-because-asserted. Two questions, two speech acts (commit vs. attest-survival), two readers, two rates of change. 019's single-question test sorts them into two files. Split condition met.

Why §14 is not already this. §14 names the falsifiable claims — F1 instrumented, F2–F5 unstaked. §14 answers a third question: "which commitments carry a falsifiable bet, and what is the bet?" The severity register answers a different one: "across all §-claims, what scrutiny was actually run, and what came through?" §14 lives in logic.md because a falsifiable bet is itself a conviction-shaped artifact (§14.2.a is literally a conviction_stake). The severity register is not conviction-shaped — it is the running ledger of attacks survived. An F-claim with a passing protocol is one row; a §-claim attacked in an epistle's tension section and held is another row; a claim never tested is a row with an empty severity cell. §14 is a subset of what the register tracks, formatted as conviction. The register is the whole surface, formatted as attestation. [→ §14.1, §14.2, §14.4]

What it rules out — the laundering it prevents. Without the register there is no way to distinguish a claim that survived severe scrutiny from one that has never been tested. Both sit in logic.md as frozen §-claims, same format, same authority. That is staleness-laundering at the conviction level (§2.4, §3.15): the appearance of authority without the substrate that earned it. The register makes the difference queryable: "show me every frozen §-claim with no severity record" is the conviction-level analog of §4.9's "show me all stakes with no empirical tests." The gap §14.4 names for F2–F5 — load-bearing, zero instrumentation — generalizes: most §-claims have never been hit hard, and the corpus does not currently show which. [→ §2.4, §3.15, §4.9, §14.4]

Lakatos: the register is what the belt did. §13.13 stakes hard core (the conviction stake, §13.1) protected by a belt — agent topology, schema, retrieval primitives, governance procedure. When pressure arrives, the belt absorbs it; the core is not touched directly. The severity register is the record of what the belt did under each push: which auxiliary claim took the hit, whether it held or was superseded, what the core would additionally need to fail for the push to reach it. §14.2.a already writes this shape for F1 (`null_result_behavior: falsifies_bet_not_conviction` names the belt claim that fails and what else must fail to threaten the core). The register generalizes that field from one F-claim to the whole corpus. Without it, §13.13's programme-level tribunal (progressive vs. degenerative) has no surface to read — "is the architecture generating or absorbing" requires reading the supersession record at scale, and the register is that record made legible. §13.13 says the diagnostic "requires reading the supersession record at scale"; the severity register is that read, standing. [→ §13.1, §13.13, §14.2]

Mayo: severity is the missing predicate. A claim earns credence by passing a severe test — one that would likely have found a flaw had one been there. logic.md records that a claim is committed-to; it does not record whether any test that could have found a flaw was run. Two claims with identical frozen status can have opposite severity histories: one survived a test built to break it, one was never probed. The register holds the discriminating field: not just "tested" but "tested how severely." A claim that survived a weak test launders credence as much as an untested one (a test that could not have found the flaw tells you nothing). The severity rating — when statable — is what carries the weight. Flag: most attempts against §-claims to date are not designed as severe tests; they are tension-section probes and shitcorping counterarguments. Whether those rise to Mayo-severity is open per-row, and the register should not inflate a survived-probe into a survived-severe-test. [→ §3.16, §13.13]

Current state — the attempts exist, scattered, un-surfaced. Falsification attempts already live in the corpus, embedded: the "rules out" clause on each §-claim (what the claim forbids — a standing falsification target); the §15 tensions (live pressure not yet resolved); the counterarguments worked through mid-shitcorping (now in the gravel records). These are real attempts. They are not organized as a cross-cutting record. They live in provenance artifacts — epistles, gravel files, tension entries — accessible only by reading each one. The register's job is to make them a surface: one place that answers, per §-claim, what was tried and what held. The raw material exists; the accessible cross-cut does not.

What a register entry looks like (candidate, not ratified). Per row: the §-claim under test → the test(s) attempted (epistle tension, shitcorping counterargument, empirical protocol, adversarial stress) → what survived / what was superseded → severity rating if statable (and explicit "not yet severely tested" when not). Mirror the §4.9 shape where it fits — a row backed by an `empirical_record` carries `measurement_protocol`, `n`, `confidence_score`; a row backed by a dialectical attempt carries the citing epistle/gravel artifact-ID and a prose because. The `tests` edge (§4.8) is the substrate spine: every register row is the readable face of a `tests` edge from an attempt-artifact to the §-claim it interrogates. Don't know the exact field set yet — same flag 019 raised for agents.md. Candidate, lifted from §4.9 and §14.2. [→ §4.8, §4.9, §14.2]

The full inventory — documents that should exist but don't. 019 made the architectural gap visible by naming it as a whole. Continuing that inventory so the gap is legible at once, not as scope for this epistle:
- **agents.md** — the who-is-this-agent roster (019). Slot specifications currently wearing conviction format inside logic.md (§6.13, §6.14, §6.17). Answers "who is this agent?"
- **glossary** — ratified at §1.12 (registers taxonomy names the domain-model register), never written. Named again in 019. Answers "what does this mean?"
- **severity register** — this epistle. Answers "how hard was this tested, what survived?"
- Candidate fourth, surfaced scanning the corpus: the **shitcorpus is the inverse register already half-built**. §3.20/§4.9/§6.6 stake `corpus_shitcorpus` — the record of reasoning *dropped* and why. The severity register is its dual: reasoning *kept* and what it survived. Via negativa (dropped) and via positiva (survived) are the two halves of the same provenance surface. Genuinely open whether they are two documents or two faces of one — flag, do not resolve. The shitcorpus is committed but unbuilt (§16.5, queued 042); the severity register is not yet even staked before this epistle. [→ §1.12, §3.20, §4.9, §6.6, §16.5]

Four questions, four-or-five files. what-does-it-mean (glossary) · who-is-this-agent (agents.md) · how-must-it-work (logic.md) · how-hard-was-it-tested (severity register) · what-was-dropped (shitcorpus, possibly fused with the register). Each splits from logic.md on 019's test: a different question for a different reader at a different rate of change. logic.md updates on ratification; the severity register updates on every test run — the fastest rate of the set, and the clearest evidence it cannot live inside a file that updates only on supersession.

Scope. Stakes the document, its relationship to logic.md, the split condition, the candidate entry shape, and the place in the full inventory. Does not implement the register. Does not assign severities to existing §-claims — that is the act of running the register, not staking it, and doing it here would inflate un-run scrutiny into recorded survival (the exact laundering the register exists to prevent). Does not resolve whether the register and the shitcorpus are one document or two. What is not yet decided: the ratified field set; whether a row is an artifact type or a derived view over `tests` edges; the cross-file citation discipline once §-numbers and severity-rows reference each other across files (019's open question, now spanning four-or-five files); what threshold of attempt counts as a severe test worth a row versus noise.
